Why Temple for Warehouse and Distribution

Temple sits on IH-35 between Austin and Waco, roughly 45 minutes north of Austin. That position puts around 28 million people within a two-and-a-half hour drive, which covers most of the Texas Triangle — Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio, and Austin.

For distribution users, the appeal is straightforward: Central Texas labour and land costs without giving up highway access. Temple also carries BNSF rail through the industrial corridor, which matters for manufacturers and bulk users who need it and is difficult to find on an available site.

The corridor has seen meaningful recent investment, including Meta’s $800M hyperscale data center campus inside Temple Industrial Park — the same park as our 1600 Eberhardt Rd facility.

What to Check When Touring Industrial Space

If you are comparing buildings in Temple, these are the specifications that usually determine whether a building actually works for an operation:

  • Clear height — governs how high you can rack and how much cube you are really leasing
  • Power — amperage and phase, and whether it is sufficient for your equipment without an upgrade
  • Divisibility — whether the building can be sized to you rather than the other way round
  • Parking ratio — frequently the binding constraint for labour-heavy or shift-based operations
  • Sprinkler type — wet, dry, or ESFR, which affects what commodities you can store
  • Backup power — a generator with automatic transfer matters for anything temperature or process sensitive
  • Highway access — not just distance to the interstate, but the truck route to reach it

We publish these numbers on each property page rather than making you request a flyer. If something you need is not listed, ask and we will tell you the actual figure.
